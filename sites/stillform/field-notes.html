LIGHTING STUDIES

What the light does

Three lighting studies, with the decisions behind each scene and the checks that matter once the fixtures are in place.

The reading roomThe long tableThe quiet courtyard

Civic interiors

The reading room

Balance the task light across shared tables as daylight falls away.

Separate the rows
Zone tables by distance from the windows. Preserve the brighter window edge during daylight and bring up the inner rows first.
Check both sides
Sit on both sides of the table with a matte page and a glossy screen. Record the view towards the fixture and the reflections you see at reading height.
Describe the scene
Specify which layer supplies light to the table, which keeps the room legible, and how the two change together after dusk.

Hospitality

The long table

Use pendants, a soft wall wash and a separate service scene.

Set the height
Start with a mockup of one place setting and a pendant at its intended height. Check the sightline across the table and across the room.
Keep routes legible
Light the route to the door separately from the tables so a dimmed dinner scene still leaves steps and corners visible.
Test the changeover
Save separate scenes for service, dinner and cleaning. Have the people who open and close the room try the controls.

Landscape

The quiet courtyard

Read the path by its edges and leave the planting in shadow.

Walk at eye level
Check the sequence from each doorway and from the approach. Keep the light sources out of the normal line of sight.
Repeat when wet
A damp paving sample reflects light differently. Check it after rain or wet the test area before choosing a finish.
Plan maintenance
Show where every fitting can be reached and aim it with planting at its expected height. Record adjustments at handover.
